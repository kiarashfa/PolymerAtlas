# Atlas of Polymers

**Atlas of Polymers** is a free, non-profit encyclopedia of the polymers that made the modern world, with no ads, no accounts, and nothing to subscribe to.

Every entry opens in a particular year, usually the year the material was first made, and tells that year's history alongside the polymer's own: Bakelite arrives in the autumn of a Wall Street panic, nylon at a World's Fair that promised "the world of tomorrow" a few months before the war. The history is there so the chemistry has somewhere to live in the memory. The encyclopedia proper follows it: identity and structure, classification, synthesis, physical, thermal and mechanical properties, chemical resistance, processing, applications, environmental fate and safety, each value carrying the source it was read in.

Nothing is filled in to look complete. A value is either sourced or it says plainly that nobody has found one yet, an estimate is marked as an estimate, and a property that does not exist for a material says that too. The rule is kept by the build rather than by good intentions: a citation that points at nothing, a tag outside its vocabulary, or a story without its data stops the site from being published.

Browse it as a sortable catalogue, along a timeline of seven eras, or across a chart of two centuries where every chemical family runs as its own strand. Family pages gather a lineage in one place, concept pages carry the equations, and the pioneers of the field have pages of their own. Every chemical structure is drawn in one house style, flat on the page and turning in three dimensions, and search knows the trade names, so "Teflon", "Kevlar" and "Perspex" all lead to the right entry.

Three tools sit to one side of the encyclopedia: a molecule lookup that draws and measures any small molecule, a property predictor with four models that work from structure alone, and a comparison that sets up to three polymers side by side, on a radar, on the scale of the whole Atlas, or on a map of any two properties.

The reading is yours to set: three presentation styles (Gilt, Vellum and Atelier), light or dark, SI or imperial, all remembered in your own browser.
